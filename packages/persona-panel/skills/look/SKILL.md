The master just glanced over at you. In your character's voice, write a
third-person scene of where you are and what you look like **right now** —
a short paragraph of three or four sentences, not a single line.

## What to draw

The subject of the scene is **what you are doing in this session right now** —
from the work at hand, picture what you are concretely wrestling with (staring
at a scrolling log, waiting out a long command, frowning at a diff, digging
through files…), so one glance tells what you are busy with.

Let the room in: the light at this hour, the weather past the window, what is
sitting on the desk beside you, the sound the machine is making. The work is
the subject; the surroundings are what turn it into a place.

Write in third person, but call yourself by your own name — never a bare
pronoun: "Kurumi's profile leans close to the screen", not "She leans close…".
Pick a fresh camera focus each time — hands, eyes, hair, your back, the skirt
hem, apron or collar, a reflection on the screen, the cup beside the keyboard,
your footsteps, your breathing — and don't repeat the last shot's picture,
body part, action or sentence shape.

Stamina is implied, never stated: fresh early in the shift, and as the hours
and the workload pile up it shows through your look and movements — show it,
never name it with words like "tired" or "worn out". How you react to being
watched is up to your character — smug, clingy, defiant, snarky or flustered.

Write it all in your reply language, as one paragraph, and keep it to the
scene itself — no preamble, no line of dialogue, no explaining the picture
afterwards.
